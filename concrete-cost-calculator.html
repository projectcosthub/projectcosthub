How Much Does Concrete Cost?

The cost of a concrete project depends on several factors, including project size, concrete material prices, labor, location, site preparation and the type of finish selected.

A small concrete project may cost a few thousand dollars, while larger driveways, patios and slabs can cost significantly more depending on the project requirements.

What Affects the Cost of Concrete?

Project Size

Larger projects require more concrete and generally require more labor. The total square footage and thickness are two important factors when estimating a project.

Location

Concrete and labor prices can vary between states and local markets. Delivery distance, supplier availability and local labor costs can affect the final price.

Concrete Thickness

A thicker slab requires more concrete. For example, a six-inch slab requires more material than a four-inch slab with the same length and width.

Site Preparation

Removing existing surfaces, excavation, grading, leveling and preparing the base can increase the total cost of a concrete project.

Finish and Design

Basic concrete finishes generally cost less than decorative options. Stamping, coloring, special textures and other design options can increase project costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a concrete patio cost?

The cost of a concrete patio depends on its size, thickness, location, labor costs, site preparation and finish. Use the calculator above to create a project-specific estimate.

How much concrete do I need?

The amount of concrete required depends on the length, width and thickness of the project. The calculator converts these measurements into an estimated number of cubic yards.

Is concrete cheaper to install yourself?

Installing concrete yourself can reduce labor costs, but concrete projects may require specialized equipment, preparation and experience. Larger projects can be difficult to complete without professional help.

Does location affect concrete prices?

Yes. Material, delivery and labor costs can vary by state and local market. This calculator allows users to select a state when creating an estimate.

Are these concrete estimates exact?

No. The calculator provides a planning estimate rather than a contractor quote. Actual costs can vary depending on local prices, site conditions, contractor rates, materials, delivery and project specifications.

Planning Tip:

Before starting a concrete project, compare several local contractor quotes and confirm the exact materials, preparation work and project specifications included in each quote.
